The three-number HRA test (old regime only)

Exemption under Section 10(13A) read with Rule 2A is the least of three figures, computed on the same period (this page uses monthly, then multiplies by 12 for the annual line):

  1. HRA actually received in the salary.
  2. Rent paid minus 10% of basic salary (DA if it counts for retirement benefits — we do not add DA unless you bake it into basic).
  3. 50% of basic if you live in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai or Kolkata; 40% of basic anywhere else. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Gurgaon are not metros for this rule.

Example, non-metro Bengaluru: basic ₹25,000, HRA ₹12,000, rent ₹15,000. (1) ₹12,000 (2) ₹15,000 − ₹2,500 = ₹12,500 (3) 40% × ₹25,000 = ₹10,000. Exemption = ₹10,000/month. Taxable HRA = ₹2,000/month. At a 30% marginal rate the annual tax saving is about ₹36,000 — which is why the “tax rate” box exists. It is a shortcut, not a full-year computation; use the income-tax calculator with the annual exemption for regime comparison.

Paperwork that actually matters

Rent receipts, rental agreement, and landlord PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh. Form 12BB is how most employers collect this. Living with parents and paying rent is allowed only if the arrangement is genuine and documented; a fake receipt is tax evasion, not a hack. The new tax regime does not allow this exemption — if you switched, the result here does not reduce TDS.
