FY 2026-27 rules — last verified 17 August 2026 Union Budget 2026 did not change personal income-tax slab rates from FY 2025-26. New-regime slabs: nil up to ₹4 lakh, then 5% / 10% / 15% / 20% / 25% / 30% in ₹4 lakh steps, with Section 87A rebate (and marginal relief) so tax is nil on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. Standard deduction modelled: ₹75,000 (new) / ₹50,000 (old) for salary or pension. Surcharge: 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore; old regime 37% above ₹5 crore (new regime capped at 25%). Plus 4% health and education cess. FY 2026-27: what this comparison actually does

Two columns, one income. The new-regime column applies the ₹75,000 standard deduction (salary/pension) and ignores 80C, 80D, HRA, and the “other deductions” box — because those deductions are not available there. The old-regime column subtracts them after the ₹50,000 standard deduction, then runs the older slabs. Both columns add surcharge (if taxable income is above ₹50 lakh) with marginal relief, then 4% cess.

Section 87A (new regime) plus marginal relief makes tax nil on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. A salaried person with ₹12.75 lakh gross and no other income is often at ₹12 lakh after the ₹75,000 standard deduction — which is why “zero tax up to ₹12.75 lakh” appears in news explainers. That is taxable income after standard deduction, not CTC.

Worked ₹18 lakh salary

New regime: taxable ₹18,00,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹17,25,000. Slab tax is ₹60,000 (up to ₹12L) + 15% of ₹4L + 20% of ₹1.25L = ₹60,000 + ₹60,000 + ₹25,000 = ₹1,45,000, plus 4% cess ≈ ₹1,50,800. Old regime with full ₹1.5 lakh 80C, ₹25,000 80D and ₹2 lakh HRA: taxable can fall near ₹13.5 lakh before 20%/30% slabs — run the boxes rather than memorising a rule of thumb. Home-loan interest u/s 24(b) belongs in “other deductions” on the old side only.

What we do not compute

Capital gains, dividend special rates, senior-citizen extra exemption under the old regime, and company-specific perquisites. Rebate 87A is not applied to special-rate income.
