GST 2.0 on an Indian invoice, both directions

From 22 September 2025 the GST Council’s GST 2.0 structure is the working set: 5% (merit), 18% (standard for most goods and services), and 40% (specified demerit / luxury). 0% remains for many exempt essentials. 3% remains for specified precious metals. The old everyday 12% and 28% slabs were largely merged into 5% / 18% / 40%. This page defaults to 18%. Pick 12 or 28 only if the tax invoice in your hand still prints that rate (use Other).

Most consumer prices in an app are GST-inclusive. A laptop sticker of ₹70,800 at 18% is not “₹70,800 plus tax”. Reverse: base = 70,800 / 1.18 = ₹60,000; GST = ₹10,800. Exclusive mode does the opposite: ₹60,000 × 18% = ₹10,800; total ₹70,800. At 40% exclusive, ₹1,00,000 of taxable value is ₹40,000 GST and ₹1,40,000 total; intra-state that GST splits as ₹20,000 CGST + ₹20,000 SGST. Inter-state is IGST at the full rate (same rupee total).

This tool does not file GSTR-1, does not look up your HSN/SAC, and does not add compensation cess. Some tobacco, pan masala, and notified goods still carry cess on top of GST — the total here will then be low. Composition dealers do not charge GST the same way; do not “add 18%” on a composition invoice.

Rate is the code, not the dropdown

Use the slab printed on the tax invoice or in the current GST rate schedule for that HSN/SAC. Confirm on the GST portal. Turnover thresholds for mandatory registration (often ₹40 lakh goods / ₹20 lakh services, lower in some special-category states) change; confirm before you register. Longer read: GST 2.0 guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

From 22 September 2025 the working slabs are 0% (exempt/nil), 5% (merit), 18% (standard), and 40% (specified demerit/luxury), plus 3% for specified precious metals. The old 12% and 28% rates were largely merged. The legal rate is still the HSN/SAC on the tax invoice.
GST exclusive means the entered amount is before tax — GST is added on top. GST inclusive means the amount already includes tax — we back-calculate the base and GST portion.
Base = Total ÷ (1 + rate/100). For 18% GST on ₹11,800 inclusive: Base = 11,800 ÷ 1.18 = ₹10,000 and GST = ₹1,800. At 40% on ₹1,40,000 inclusive: base = 1,40,000 ÷ 1.40 = ₹1,00,000.
For intra-state sales, GST splits equally into CGST and SGST (9% + 9% at 18%; 20% + 20% at 40%). For inter-state, IGST applies at the full rate. The rupee GST amount is the same; only the label changes.
Service suppliers often must register above ₹20 lakh turnover (₹10 lakh in some special-category states). Goods suppliers often have a higher threshold. Confirm the live limit on the GST portal before you charge tax — this page does not decide registration.
18% is the standard rate for most goods and services. 5% is the merit rate for many essentials. 40% is a narrow demerit/luxury band. Milk and many fresh foods stay nil or exempt. Cess items sit outside this tool. Always read the HSN/SAC, not this FAQ.
