What an EMI actually contains

Indian home, car, and personal loans almost always use a reducing-balance EMI: the instalment is fixed, but the split between interest and principal changes every month. Month 1 of a ₹50 lakh loan at 8.5% for 20 years is roughly ₹35,417 interest and only ₹7,974 principal. By year 15 the interest share has fallen sharply. That is why a prepayment early in the loan saves more than the same rupee amount in year 18.

This calculator uses EMI = P × r × (1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where r is the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is tenure in months. Banks may still differ by a few rupees because of day-count conventions, processing fees bundled into the loan, or rounding.

Tenure is the expensive lever

For a ₹30 lakh home loan at 8.5%:

TenureEMITotal interest
10 years≈ ₹37,186≈ ₹14.6 lakh
15 years≈ ₹29,542≈ ₹23.2 lakh
20 years≈ ₹26,035≈ ₹32.5 lakh
25 years≈ ₹24,180≈ ₹42.5 lakh

Stretching 10 years to 25 years cuts EMI by about ₹13,000 but more than doubles interest. If the lower EMI is the only way the loan is affordable, take it — then prepay toward tenure when you get a bonus, not toward a lower EMI, unless cash-flow is the constraint.

What this tool does not include

Stamp duty, registration, GST on some charges, credit-protect insurance, and floating-rate resets are outside the formula. FOIR (total EMIs ÷ net income) at most banks sits near 40–50%.
