GST Guide India 2026 — GST 2.0 Slabs, Calculation & Registration

5%, 18% and 40% after 22 September 2025 — not the old 12% / 28% list

Written by Rizwan Ali · Last updated 18 August 2026 · Confirm HSN/SAC on the GST portal

A 2026 GST page that still lists 12% and 28% as the everyday slabs is describing 2024. The GST Council’s GST 2.0 rate structure took effect from 22 September 2025: two primary rates — 5% (merit) and 18% (standard) — plus a 40% demerit/luxury band, with 0% for many exempt essentials and 3% for specified precious metals. Most items that used to sit at 12% or 28% moved into 5%, 18%, or 40%.

This guide is for freelancers, small shops, and salaried people with side income who need to add or peel GST off an invoice. It is an estimate of how the arithmetic works, not a substitute for the HSN/SAC on your tax invoice or a GST practitioner. Use it with the GST calculator.

GST 2.0 slabs (from 22 September 2025)

Rate Role Typical use (illustrative — check HSN/SAC)
0% Exempt / nil Many unbranded essentials, specified health and education items
3% Niche Specified precious metals (gold, silver) — not “everything jewellery”
5% Merit Many everyday / mass-consumption goods after rationalisation
18% Standard Most goods and services, including many items that used to be 12% or 28%
40% Demerit / luxury A narrow notified set (luxury / sin). Some goods still carry cess on top

Do not memorise a blog’s item list. Classification follows the current CBIC / GST Council schedule for that HSN or SAC. Compensation cess can still apply on notified goods; this guide’s rupee examples ignore cess.

What happened to 12% and 28%?

They were the middle and top bands of the old five-tier system (0 / 5 / 12 / 18 / 28). GST 2.0 collapsed that stack. If an old invoice or an old calculator still prints 12% or 28%, treat it as legacy unless the live tax invoice in your hand still shows that rate. Our calculator keeps an “Other” box for that case; it does not treat 12% or 28% as current defaults.

Forward GST (exclusive)

GST = taxable value × rate / 100. Total = taxable value + GST.

  • ₹10,000 service at 18% → GST ₹1,800, invoice ₹11,800. Intra-state: CGST ₹900 + SGST ₹900.
  • ₹1,00,000 taxable at 40% → GST ₹40,000, invoice ₹1,40,000. Intra-state: CGST ₹20,000 + SGST ₹20,000. Inter-state: IGST ₹40,000 (same total).

Reverse GST (inclusive / sticker price)

Base = total / (1 + rate/100). GST = total − base. B2C apps usually show inclusive prices.

  • ₹11,800 inclusive at 18%: base = 11,800 / 1.18 = ₹10,000.
  • ₹1,40,000 inclusive at 40%: base = 1,40,000 / 1.40 = ₹1,00,000.

If you reverse at the wrong rate (using 28% on a 2026 18% sticker), both the base and the ITC claim are wrong.

Registration (still check the portal)

Thresholds are notified and can move. In most states the common working figures remain around ₹40 lakh annual turnover for goods and ₹20 lakh for services, with lower limits in some special-category states. E-commerce operators and many inter-state suppliers face different triggers. GSTIN is what lets you issue a proper tax invoice and claim input tax credit. Salary income alone does not require registration.

Input tax credit

Registered businesses offset GST paid on inward supplies against GST collected on outward supplies, subject to the Act (valid tax invoice, supplier compliance, blocked credits). Unregistered freelancers cannot claim ITC — that cost sits in your price. Composition dealers generally cannot claim ITC the same way as the regular scheme.

Composition scheme

A simplified option for small businesses under a notified turnover cap (commonly discussed around ₹1.5 crore for goods, with separate service rules). You pay a prescribed rate on turnover, with limited ITC and invoice restrictions. Do not run a regular 18% add-on on a composition bill. Confirm eligibility and the current composition rates in the latest notification — they are not the 5 / 18 / 40 GST 2.0 consumer slabs.

Use this arithmetic when

  • You have a taxable value or an inclusive sticker and a known rate
  • You need CGST/SGST halves or an IGST equivalent
  • You are teaching yourself GST 2.0 vs an old 12/28 calculator

Do not use it as

  • An HSN lookup or a GSTR-1 filing tool
  • A cess calculator (tobacco, pan masala, some vehicles)
  • A composition-scheme tax engine

Sources: GST Council / CBIC GST 2.0 rate structure effective 22 September 2025; gst.gov.in. Last verified 18 August 2026. Item-wise rates move; this page will be wrong the day a new notification shifts your HSN.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are GST slabs in India in 2026?

Under GST 2.0 (from 22 September 2025) the working slabs are 0% (exempt/nil), 5% (merit), 18% (standard for most goods and services), and 40% (specified demerit/luxury), plus 3% for specified precious metals. The old 12% and 28% slabs were largely merged. The legal rate is the HSN/SAC on the tax invoice.

When must I register for GST?

In most states, mandatory registration is commonly triggered around ₹40 lakh annual turnover for goods or ₹20 lakh for services, with lower thresholds in some special-category states. E-commerce sellers and inter-state suppliers have separate rules. Confirm the live limit on the GST portal before you charge tax.

How to calculate GST inclusive price?

Base amount = Total / (1 + rate/100). For 18% inclusive ₹11,800: base = 11,800/1.18 = ₹10,000, GST = ₹1,800. For 40% inclusive ₹1,40,000: base = 1,40,000/1.40 = ₹1,00,000, GST = ₹40,000.

CGST vs IGST — difference?

Intra-state sales: CGST + SGST split equally (9%+9% at 18%; 20%+20% at 40%). Inter-state: IGST at the full rate. The rupee GST amount is the same; only which government receives it changes.

Do salaried employees need GST registration?

Salary income alone does not require GST. You may need to register if a side business or freelance turnover crosses the threshold, you supply through e-commerce, or you opt in voluntarily for input tax credit.
